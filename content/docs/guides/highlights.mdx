After every CS2 map, Auto Tournament reads the demo, picks each player's best moments (multi-kills, clutches, the odd funny one) and has them recorded from the player's own eyes: a clip per moment, a reel per player, a reel per match and team, and one for the tournament. Players find theirs on their profile; everyone can watch them on the match page.

The recording happens on **recorders**: Linux PCs with a GPU, CS2 and Steam that ask the platform for work. Add as many as you like; they share it.

## How it works

1. A map ends and its demo arrives (Ready Up uploads it).
2. The **demo worker** (the `auto-tournament-worker` container next to the platform) reads it: stats, the 2D replay, and the moments.
3. A **recorder** takes the moments of a map, plays the demo in CS2 and records each one frame by frame with [Frame by Frame](/guides/frame-by-frame), then uploads the clips and the player's reel.
4. When a match is all recorded, the match and team reels are joined from the clips.

## Settings

**Manage → Highlights → CS2 → Settings**:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Video quality | **Smooth** (recommended): 720p at 240 fps, so slow motion at 0.25× still plays at 60 frames a second, and clips stay small enough to stream. **Crisp**: 1080p at 120 fps. **LAN, maximum**: 1440p at 240 fps and 60 Mbit/s, for events watched on a local network. **Custom**: your own resolution, frame rate and bitrate. Leave the bitrate on Automatic unless you have a reason not to. |
| Clips per player per map | 1 to 6. Each takes a recorder a few minutes. |
| Auto Tournament logo | A faint logo in the corner. |
| Music under reels | Every track, only the ones you pick, or none. You add the tracks (Auto Tournament ships none). |
| Keep clean copies | Keeps each clip without its overlay too, so overlays can be redrawn and reels made from clean clips. |

## Add a recorder

Open **Manage → Highlights → CS2 → Recorders** and click **Add a recorder**. Pick what the machine is; the dialog makes a recorder key and puts it in a command to copy. The key is shown once: open the dialog again for the next machine. Old keys can be revoked under **Recorder keys**.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Gaming PC

A Linux PC with Docker, Steam (signed in) and CS2 installed, and an NVIDIA or AMD GPU. Paste the command in a terminal in its desktop session. It starts the recorder container, which uses the PC's GPU and finds CS2 in its Steam libraries. It starts again whenever Docker does.

Switch Steam to offline mode: demo playback needs no online session, so every recorder can use the same Steam account. The recorder takes Steam online by itself only to update CS2 (see [CS2 updates](#cs2-updates)).

</Step>

<Step>

### Server (headless)

An Ubuntu 24.04 server with an NVIDIA GPU, no desktop needed. The command has two lines:

1. Once, with `sudo`: installs the NVIDIA driver and Docker and turns sleep off. Reboot if it says so.
2. Starts Steam and CS2 in a container, with the recorder in it. Run it again later to update.

Then, once: open the web UI it prints (https, port `8483`), sign in with the first-run login it prints and set your own. Sign Steam in (the first sign-in can say "waiting for network" for a minute), set it to offline mode and install CS2. The recorder starts taking work when CS2 is there.

</Step>

<Step>

### Check it on the Recorders page

The recorder shows up under its own name the first time it asks for work; rename it in the list. On its first job it runs a short **benchmark** (one moment at 240 and 120 Hz, timed and checked for smoothness) and keeps the fastest smooth setting. The list shows its GPU, the benchmark, seconds per clip and every run with its log. A warning shows there when a CS2 update has turned [Frame by Frame](/guides/frame-by-frame#after-a-cs2-update) off. **Update all** updates every recorder to the platform's version.

</Step>

</Steps>

Windows isn't supported yet: the recorder captures CS2 through gamescope, which is Linux only.

## Playing on a recorder

Don't play or run other GPU work on a recorder while it records: the GPU is shared and the clips stutter. Every clip is checked for repeated frames (a stutter): at 4% or more the platform turns it down and has it recorded again, preferably on another recorder. A recorder with three turned-down clips in a row gets no work for 15 minutes and shows as **Paused**; **Resume** gives it work again sooner.

## CS2 updates

A recorder keeps its CS2 up to date by itself. While it has no work, it asks Steam every 30 minutes whether CS2 is current. When it's behind, the recorder takes Steam online with its saved login, waits for the update to finish (up to an hour), and puts Steam back in offline mode. It takes no work meanwhile, and the Recorders page shows **updating CS2**.

If Steam can't sign in (the saved login expired), the run log says so: sign Steam in again on the recorder's desktop. To update CS2 yourself instead, set `AT_CS2_UPDATE=0` on the recorder.

## Recording clips again

On **Manage → Highlights → CS2 → Clips**, **Redo** records a clip or reel again, and **Make the outdated ones again** redoes everything made at older settings. A redo keeps the old video until the new one is ready:

- **Keep the old videos** stops the redos still waiting and keeps what they had.
- A redo that fails all three attempts keeps its old video too.
- A clip that failed and has no video holds its reels back, so a reel is never made without a match's best plays. Redo it, or drop it on the Clips page.

A job has no time limit, so a recorder can work through a long match for hours. Only a job that makes no progress for 30 minutes is stopped and tried again.

## Matches played somewhere else

**All matches → Import a match** adds a match played elsewhere (another platform, a LAN without Auto Tournament) from its demos, one per map in the order they were played. The teams, the players and the score are read from the demos; teams and players that are new are created. Then the match gets stats, the 2D replay and highlights like any other.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| *Failed to connect to wayland socket* in a run log | The container needs the PC's display: `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and the `/run/user/<uid>` mount. |
| A recorder is Paused | Its clips stuttered: something else was using the GPU, or the refresh rate is too high. Close the game or run the benchmark again. |
| No recorder picks anything up | Its key (revoked?), whether its group is enabled, and its last run's log on the Recorders page. |
| Clips take long | The resolution and frame rate in Settings, and the recorder's GPU (its seconds per clip on the Recorders page). After a CS2 update, check the Recorders page for a [Frame by Frame](/guides/frame-by-frame#after-a-cs2-update) warning. |
| Clips stop to buffer while they play | The bitrate is higher than the viewer's connection. Use Smooth, or a lower bitrate under Custom. The first view of each clip comes from your machine, so a slow upload buffers too. Behind Cloudflare, add the [cache rule](/guides/reverse-proxy#cloudflare). |
| Slow motion looks choppy | Clips at 60 fps play at 15 frames a second at 0.25×. Use 240 fps (Smooth), then **Make the outdated ones again** on the Clips page. |
