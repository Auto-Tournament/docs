After every CS2 map, Auto Tournament reads the demo, picks each player's best moments (multi-kills, clutches, the odd funny one) and has them recorded from the player's own eyes: a clip per moment, a reel per player, a reel per match and team, and one for the tournament. Players find theirs on their profile; everyone can watch them on the match page.

The recording happens on **recorders**: Linux PCs with a GPU, CS2 and Steam that ask the platform for work. Add as many as you like; they share it.

## How it works

1. A map ends and its demo arrives (Ready Up uploads it).
2. The **demo worker** (the `auto-tournament-worker` container next to the platform) reads it: stats, the 2D replay, and the moments.
3. A **recorder** takes the moments of a map, plays the demo in CS2 and records each one, then uploads the clips and the player's reel.
4. When a match is all recorded, the match and team reels are joined from the clips.

## Settings

**CS2 → Highlights → Settings**:

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Resolution and frame rate | What the recorders make, up to 4K and 240 fps. Smaller and fewer frames record faster: 720p at 30 fps takes about half as long as 1440p at 120 fps. |
| Clips per player per map | 1 to 6. Each takes a recorder a few minutes. |
| Auto Tournament logo | A faint logo in the corner. |
| Music under reels | Every track, only the ones you pick, or none. You add the tracks (Auto Tournament ships none). |
| Keep clean copies | Keeps each clip without its overlay too, so overlays can be redrawn and reels made from clean clips. |

## Add a recorder

<Steps>

<Step>

### A Linux PC with Steam and CS2

Install Steam, sign in once and install CS2. Then switch Steam to offline mode: demo playback needs no online session, so every recorder can use the same Steam account.

Windows isn't supported yet: the recorder captures CS2 through gamescope, which is Linux only.

</Step>

<Step>

### Run the recorder container

As the PC's own user (it uses your GPU, display, sound, Steam and CS2):

```bash
docker run -d --name at-recorder --restart unless-stopped \
  --user "$(id -u):$(id -g)" --group-add video --group-add render \
  --device /dev/dri --ipc=host --net=host --shm-size 4g \
  --security-opt seccomp=unconfined --cap-add SYS_NICE \
  -v "$HOME:$HOME" -v "/run/user/$(id -u):/run/user/$(id -u)" -v /tmp:/tmp \
  -e HOME="$HOME" -e XDG_RUNTIME_DIR="/run/user/$(id -u)" \
  -e WAYLAND_DISPLAY="$WAYLAND_DISPLAY" \
  -e DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS="unix:path=/run/user/$(id -u)/bus" \
  -e AT_URL=https://tournament.example.com \
  -e AT_WORKER_TOKEN=<one of API_TOKENS in the platform's .env> \
  -e AT_WORKER_NAME="$(hostname)" \
  -e AT_CS2_GAME="$HOME/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Counter-Strike Global Offensive/game" \
  -e AT_SNIPER_RUN="$HOME/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/SteamLinuxRuntime_sniper/run" \
  sivertio/auto-tournament-recorder:next
```

- **NVIDIA**: add `--gpus all` (with the NVIDIA Container Toolkit).
- **CS2 on another disk**: mount it too (`-v /mnt/games:/mnt/games`) and point `AT_CS2_GAME` at it.
- `AT_WORKER_NAME` is the name on the Highlights page; the recorder keeps its benchmark and history under it.

</Step>

<Step>

### Check it on the Highlights page

On its first job the recorder runs a short **benchmark**: one moment at 240 and 120 Hz, timed and checked for smoothness. It keeps the fastest smooth setting. Then it shows up under **CS2 → Highlights → Recorders** with its GPU, its benchmark, seconds per clip, and every run with its log.

</Step>

</Steps>

## Playing on a recorder

You can play on a PC while it records, but the GPU is shared. Every clip is checked for repeated frames (a stutter): at 4% or more the platform turns it down and has it recorded again, preferably on another recorder. A recorder with three turned-down clips in a row gets no work for 15 minutes and shows as **Paused**; **Resume** gives it work again sooner.

## Matches played somewhere else

**All matches → Import a match** adds a match played elsewhere (another platform, a LAN without Auto Tournament) from its demos, one per map in the order they were played. The teams, the players and the score are read from the demos; teams and players that are new are created. Then the match gets stats, the 2D replay and highlights like any other.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| *Failed to connect to wayland socket* in a run log | The container needs the PC's display: `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and the `/run/user/<uid>` mount. |
| A recorder is Paused | Its clips stuttered: something else was using the GPU, or the refresh rate is too high. Close the game or run the benchmark again. |
| No recorder picks anything up | The recorder's `AT_URL` and token; its last run's log on the Highlights page. |
| Clips take long | The resolution and frame rate in Settings, and the recorder's GPU (its seconds per clip on the Highlights page). |
