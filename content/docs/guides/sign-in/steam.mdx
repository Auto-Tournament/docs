Users sign in with their Steam account. Games that run on Steam, such as CS2, need it: only players with a Steam account connected are put on those servers. Steam is optional for everything else. See [Sign-in providers](/guides/sign-in) for how accounts and admin access work.

<Steps>

<Step>

### Get a Steam Web API key

1. Open the [Steam Web API key page](https://steamcommunity.com/dev/apikey) and sign in with Steam.
2. In **Domain Name**, enter the domain of your install, for example `tournament.example.com`. Steam does not check it, so `localhost` also works.
3. Tick the box to agree to the Steam Web API Terms of Use.
4. Click **Register**.
5. Copy the **Key**.

Steam does not give keys to limited accounts. An account is limited until it has spent at least 5 USD in the Steam store.

</Step>

<Step>

### Turn it on in Auto Tournament

In **Settings → Sign-in → Steam**, paste the key, turn it on and save, then click **Test**. The **Sign in with Steam** button appears on the login page right away.

You do not register a redirect URL at Steam: Auto Tournament sends Steam the return address itself (`/api/auth/steam/callback` on your address). Make sure `FRONTEND_BASE_URL` is the address you open in the browser.

Prefer environment variables? Set `STEAM_API_KEY` (and `AUTH_STEAM_ENABLED=false` to turn Steam off) and restart. They are imported once.

</Step>

<Step>

### Test the button

Open `/login` in a private browser window, click **Sign in with Steam** and sign in. Someone new gets an account of their own when registration is open (**Settings → Players & Access**).

If the button is missing, the key is empty or Steam rejected it: Auto Tournament hides the button when the key does not work. **Test** in Settings says which.

</Step>

</Steps>
